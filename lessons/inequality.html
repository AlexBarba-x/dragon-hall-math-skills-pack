Lesson 2 of 4

Words to an inequality

You will turn a sentence about a quotient into an inequality, with the numbers in the right order.

Goal

Write the quotient the correct way around. Choose the sign that matches less than (not “at most”). Read the symbols back into words as a check.

Commons lessons cited: DH-MATH-6-EE-A-2-write-L001 (Writing Expressions from Words) and DH-MATH-6-EE-B-8-L001 (Inequalities on the Number Line). Concept checks: DH-MATH-6-EE-A-2-write-CC, DH-MATH-6-EE-B-8-CC.

Concrete · the letter is a length

Say This strip is \(n\). We do not know its length, so we give it a name. An expression is a record of what we did to \(n\).

Do Imagine cutting the \(n\) strip into 5 equal pieces. That record is the quotient of \(n\) and 5.

Listen for \(n\) is the amount being cut, so \(n\) goes on top: \(\dfrac{n}{5}\).

Pictorial · two traps

Say Division has a trap. “The quotient of \(t\) and 3.” Which number gets divided up? That one goes on top. “Divide 12 by \(k\)” puts 12 on top.

Do Write both: the quotient of \(t\) and 3, and divide 12 by \(k\).

Listen for \(\dfrac{t}{3}\) and \(\dfrac{12}{k}\). Wrong answers to catch: \(\dfrac{3}{t}\) and \(\dfrac{k}{12}\).

Say Now the inequality words. “Less than 8” is a strict door. 8 itself is left out. We write \(\lt 8\), not \(\le 8\).

Do Test the boundary. Is 8 less than 8?

Listen for No. So the boundary is not a solution.

Abstract · read it back

Say Put the two moves together. “The quotient of a number \(n\) and 5 is less than 8.” Write it, then read it back.

Do Write the inequality, then say it in words.

Listen for \(\dfrac{n}{5} \lt 8\). Read-back: “\(n\) split into 5 parts is less than 8.” That matches. \(\dfrac{5}{n} \lt 8\) would mean 5 is the amount being cut.

Worked example

Write an inequality: the quotient of a number \(n\) and 5 is less than 8.

  1. Quotient of \(n\) and 5 means \(n\) is divided: \(\dfrac{n}{5}\).
  2. “Less than 8” is strict: \(\lt 8\).
  3. Together: \(\dfrac{n}{5} \lt 8\).
  4. Read back. The words match, so the order is right.

Interactive check

1. “The quotient of a number \(w\) and 6 is less than 4.” Which inequality is right?

2. “The quotient of 12 and a number \(k\) is less than 3.” Type the inequality.

Adult only — reveal keys

1. \(\dfrac{w}{6} \lt 4\). \(w\) is being cut. “Less than” is not “at most.”

2. \(\dfrac{12}{k} \lt 3\). 12 is the amount being divided.
