Lesson 3 of 4

Integer exponent laws

You will simplify a product and a quotient of powers of the same base, including a zero exponent.

Goal

Read an exponent as a count of factors. Multiply by adding counts. Divide by subtracting counts. Show why a nonzero base to the power 0 must be 1, so the product law keeps working.

Commons lesson cited: DH-MATH-8-EE-A-1-L001 (Where the Exponent Laws Come From). Concept check: DH-MATH-8-EE-A-1-CC.

Concrete · count the factors

Say For a positive whole number \(n\), \(a^n\) means \(a\) multiplied by itself \(n\) times. An exponent is a count.

Do Write \(a^4 \cdot a^3\) out as factors and count.

Listen for Four factors joined with three factors make seven factors, so \(a^4 \cdot a^3 = a^7\). In general \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\).

Concrete · division is counting backwards

Say In \(\dfrac{a^5}{a^2}\), two factors on the bottom cancel two of the five on top, leaving three. The exponents subtract.

Do Write \(\dfrac{a^6}{a^4}\) in factors and cancel.

Listen for \(a^2\). You do not have to memorize if you can count.

Pictorial · why the zero exponent is 1

Say What should \(a^0\) mean? Multiply \(a\) by itself zero times does not describe anything. Mathematicians give zero the meaning that keeps the product law true.

Do Finish this: \(a^m \cdot a^0\) must equal \(a^{m+0}\), which is just \(a^m\). What number can you multiply by without changing anything?

Listen for 1. So \(a^0 = 1\) for any nonzero \(a\). It is not 0.

Abstract · same base, then simplify

Say These laws are about multiplying and dividing powers of the same base. They say nothing about sums. Check: \((2+3)^2 = 25\), but \(2^2 + 3^2 = 13\).

Do Simplify \(\dfrac{4^5 \cdot 4^0}{4^2}\) by naming the law at each step.

Listen for Product law: \(4^{5+0} = 4^5\). Quotient law: \(4^{5-2} = 4^3 = 64\). Zero exponent made a factor of 1.

Worked example

Simplify \(\dfrac{4^5 \cdot 4^0}{4^2}\).

  1. Same base 4, so the laws apply.
  2. \(4^0 = 1\), and \(4^5 \cdot 1 = 4^5\) (or add: \(5 + 0 = 5\)).
  3. Divide: \(5 - 2 = 3\), so \(4^3\).
  4. \(4^3 = 64\).

Interactive check

1. Simplify \(\dfrac{7^3 \cdot 7^4}{7^0}\) as a single power of 7.

2. Simplify \(\dfrac{2^8}{2^3 \cdot 2^0}\) as a number or as \(2^5\).

Adult only — reveal keys

1. \(7^{3+4-0} = 7^7\). The zero exponent does not wipe the other factors out.

2. \(2^{8-3-0} = 2^5 = 32\).

Catch: multiplying exponents instead of adding, or saying \(a^0 = 0\).
