Lesson 1 of 4

Signed mixed numbers

You will add a negative mixed number to a positive mixed number when the parts have different bottom numbers.

Goal

Convert each mixed number. Glue every sign to its own number. Rewrite the parts in one common unit. Then add. You should be able to say why the sign travels with the number.

Commons lessons cited: DH-MATH-5-NF-A-1-mixed-L001 (Wholes and Parts Together) and DH-MATH-7-NS-A-1d-L001 (Rearranging a Sum on Purpose). Concept checks: DH-MATH-5-NF-A-1-mixed-CC, DH-MATH-7-NS-A-1d-CC.

Concrete · gather wholes, recut parts

Say Put two mixed numbers on the mat in your mind. Gather wholes with wholes. The parts only add after they are the same size. If a half meets five eighths, trade the half for four eighths first.

Do On paper, draw two rows: a wholes row and a parts row. For \(2\frac{1}{2} + 1\frac{5}{8}\), write wholes \(2 + 1\) and parts \(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{5}{8}\).

Listen for Four eighths and five eighths make nine eighths. Eight of those snap into one more whole, with one eighth left. The sum is \(4\frac{1}{8}\).

Pictorial · the sign is glued on

Say Now one mixed number is negative. The two addition laws talk about adding only. Rewrite first so every sign rides with its number. A minus in front of a mixed number belongs to the whole value, not just the whole-number part.

Do Rewrite \(-2\frac{1}{3} + 1\frac{5}{6}\) as adding a negative mixed number to a positive one. Turn each mixed number into one signed fraction.

Listen for \(-2\frac{1}{3} = -\dfrac{7}{3}\) and \(1\frac{5}{6} = \dfrac{11}{6}\). The minus stays in front of seven thirds. It does not get left behind.

If stuck Ask: which number does that minus belong to? Then insist on the rewrite as the first line.

Abstract · common unit, then signed sum

Say Thirds and sixths are different sizes. Recut both fractions into sixths, then add the signed parts. Scan before you grind: after the rewrite, the chain is a pure sum.

Do Convert \(-\dfrac{7}{3}\) to sixths and add \(\dfrac{11}{6}\).

Listen for \(-\dfrac{14}{6} + \dfrac{11}{6} = -\dfrac{3}{6} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\).

Worked example

Compute \(-2\frac{1}{3} + 1\frac{5}{6}\).

  1. Rewrite as signed impropers: \(-\dfrac{7}{3} + \dfrac{11}{6}\).
  2. Common unit sixths: \(-\dfrac{14}{6} + \dfrac{11}{6}\).
  3. Add the numerators: \(-14 + 11 = -3\), so \(-\dfrac{3}{6}\).
  4. Simplify: \(-\dfrac{1}{2}\).

The rows way agrees: wholes \(-2 + 1 = -1\), parts \(-\dfrac{2}{6} + \dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{3}{6}\), then \(-1 + \dfrac{1}{2} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\).

Interactive check

Write your answer, then check. A grown-up opens the reveal only if you need it.

1. Compute \(-3\frac{1}{4} + 2\frac{5}{8}\). Give a simplified fraction.

2. Compute \(-1\frac{2}{5} + 2\frac{1}{2}\). Mixed number or improper fraction is fine.

Adult only — reveal keys

1. \(-\dfrac{13}{4}+\dfrac{21}{8}=-\dfrac{26}{8}+\dfrac{21}{8}=-\dfrac{5}{8}\).

2. \(-\dfrac{7}{5}+\dfrac{5}{2}=-\dfrac{14}{10}+\dfrac{25}{10}=\dfrac{11}{10}=1\frac{1}{10}\).

Watch for adding unlike parts without converting, or dropping the sign when the number is rewritten.

Lesson 2 of 4

Words to an inequality

You will turn a sentence about a quotient into an inequality, with the numbers in the right order.

Goal

Write the quotient the correct way around. Choose the sign that matches less than (not “at most”). Read the symbols back into words as a check.

Commons lessons cited: DH-MATH-6-EE-A-2-write-L001 (Writing Expressions from Words) and DH-MATH-6-EE-B-8-L001 (Inequalities on the Number Line). Concept checks: DH-MATH-6-EE-A-2-write-CC, DH-MATH-6-EE-B-8-CC.

Concrete · the letter is a length

Say This strip is \(n\). We do not know its length, so we give it a name. An expression is a record of what we did to \(n\).

Do Imagine cutting the \(n\) strip into 5 equal pieces. That record is the quotient of \(n\) and 5.

Listen for \(n\) is the amount being cut, so \(n\) goes on top: \(\dfrac{n}{5}\).

Pictorial · two traps

Say Division has a trap. “The quotient of \(t\) and 3.” Which number gets divided up? That one goes on top. “Divide 12 by \(k\)” puts 12 on top.

Do Write both: the quotient of \(t\) and 3, and divide 12 by \(k\).

Listen for \(\dfrac{t}{3}\) and \(\dfrac{12}{k}\). Wrong answers to catch: \(\dfrac{3}{t}\) and \(\dfrac{k}{12}\).

Say Now the inequality words. “Less than 8” is a strict door. 8 itself is left out. We write \(\lt 8\), not \(\le 8\).

Do Test the boundary. Is 8 less than 8?

Listen for No. So the boundary is not a solution.

Abstract · read it back

Say Put the two moves together. “The quotient of a number \(n\) and 5 is less than 8.” Write it, then read it back.

Do Write the inequality, then say it in words.

Listen for \(\dfrac{n}{5} \lt 8\). Read-back: “\(n\) split into 5 parts is less than 8.” That matches. \(\dfrac{5}{n} \lt 8\) would mean 5 is the amount being cut.

Worked example

Write an inequality: the quotient of a number \(n\) and 5 is less than 8.

  1. Quotient of \(n\) and 5 means \(n\) is divided: \(\dfrac{n}{5}\).
  2. “Less than 8” is strict: \(\lt 8\).
  3. Together: \(\dfrac{n}{5} \lt 8\).
  4. Read back. The words match, so the order is right.

Interactive check

1. “The quotient of a number \(w\) and 6 is less than 4.” Which inequality is right?

2. “The quotient of 12 and a number \(k\) is less than 3.” Type the inequality.

Adult only — reveal keys

1. \(\dfrac{w}{6} \lt 4\). \(w\) is being cut. “Less than” is not “at most.”

2. \(\dfrac{12}{k} \lt 3\). 12 is the amount being divided.

Lesson 3 of 4

Integer exponent laws

You will simplify a product and a quotient of powers of the same base, including a zero exponent.

Goal

Read an exponent as a count of factors. Multiply by adding counts. Divide by subtracting counts. Show why a nonzero base to the power 0 must be 1, so the product law keeps working.

Commons lesson cited: DH-MATH-8-EE-A-1-L001 (Where the Exponent Laws Come From). Concept check: DH-MATH-8-EE-A-1-CC.

Concrete · count the factors

Say For a positive whole number \(n\), \(a^n\) means \(a\) multiplied by itself \(n\) times. An exponent is a count.

Do Write \(a^4 \cdot a^3\) out as factors and count.

Listen for Four factors joined with three factors make seven factors, so \(a^4 \cdot a^3 = a^7\). In general \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\).

Concrete · division is counting backwards

Say In \(\dfrac{a^5}{a^2}\), two factors on the bottom cancel two of the five on top, leaving three. The exponents subtract.

Do Write \(\dfrac{a^6}{a^4}\) in factors and cancel.

Listen for \(a^2\). You do not have to memorize if you can count.

Pictorial · why the zero exponent is 1

Say What should \(a^0\) mean? Multiply \(a\) by itself zero times does not describe anything. Mathematicians give zero the meaning that keeps the product law true.

Do Finish this: \(a^m \cdot a^0\) must equal \(a^{m+0}\), which is just \(a^m\). What number can you multiply by without changing anything?

Listen for 1. So \(a^0 = 1\) for any nonzero \(a\). It is not 0.

Abstract · same base, then simplify

Say These laws are about multiplying and dividing powers of the same base. They say nothing about sums. Check: \((2+3)^2 = 25\), but \(2^2 + 3^2 = 13\).

Do Simplify \(\dfrac{4^5 \cdot 4^0}{4^2}\) by naming the law at each step.

Listen for Product law: \(4^{5+0} = 4^5\). Quotient law: \(4^{5-2} = 4^3 = 64\). Zero exponent made a factor of 1.

Worked example

Simplify \(\dfrac{4^5 \cdot 4^0}{4^2}\).

  1. Same base 4, so the laws apply.
  2. \(4^0 = 1\), and \(4^5 \cdot 1 = 4^5\) (or add: \(5 + 0 = 5\)).
  3. Divide: \(5 - 2 = 3\), so \(4^3\).
  4. \(4^3 = 64\).

Interactive check

1. Simplify \(\dfrac{7^3 \cdot 7^4}{7^0}\) as a single power of 7.

2. Simplify \(\dfrac{2^8}{2^3 \cdot 2^0}\) as a number or as \(2^5\).

Adult only — reveal keys

1. \(7^{3+4-0} = 7^7\). The zero exponent does not wipe the other factors out.

2. \(2^{8-3-0} = 2^5 = 32\).

Catch: multiplying exponents instead of adding, or saying \(a^0 = 0\).

Lesson 4 of 4

Base pay and percent of sales

You will find earnings from sales, and sales from earnings, when pay is a fixed amount plus a percent of sales.

Goal

Reduce the job to \(\text{part} = \dfrac{p}{100} \times \text{base}\). Name the base of the percent every time. Then solve both directions.

Commons lesson cited: DH-MATH-7-RP-A-3-L001 (One Principle for Every Percent). Concept check: DH-MATH-7-RP-A-3-CC.

Concrete · name the base first

Say Before you compute anything, ask the question every percent problem turns on: percent of what? A percent is a rate. \(p\%\) means \(p\) out of every 100, the number \(\dfrac{p}{100}\).

Do Place a paper tag that says BASE on the amount the percent acts on. In “$280 plus 5% of sales,” the percent acts on sales, not on the $280.

Listen for Base of the 5% is the sales amount. Base pay is a separate addend.

Pictorial · write the equation

Say Earnings = base pay + rate × sales. That is \(E = 280 + 0.05S\). The 0.05 is 5% written as a rate.

Do If Saturday sales are $640, compute the commission first, then add the base pay.

Listen for \(0.05 \times 640 = 32\), then \(280 + 32 = 312\). Earnings are $312.

Abstract · the unknown is the base

Say When the unknown is the sales, the same equation becomes something to solve. Nothing new is invented. Part = rate × base, with sales as the base.

Do Maya earned $400. \(280 + 0.05S = 400\). Find \(S\).

Listen for \(0.05S = 120\), so \(S = 2400\). Sales were $2,400. Listen for the equation before the answer.

If stuck Undo the base pay first. What remains is the percent piece. Then divide by the rate, not by 5.

Worked example

Maya earns $280 plus 5% of her sales.

  1. Name the base: the percent acts on sales \(S\). Equation: \(E = 280 + 0.05S\).
  2. Given sales $640: \(E = 280 + 0.05 \times 640 = 280 + 32 = 312\).
  3. Given earnings $400: \(280 + 0.05S = 400\), so \(0.05S = 120\), \(S = 2400\).

Interactive check

Same job in both items: $350 plus 8% of sales.

1. Sales are $1,250. What are the earnings, in dollars?

2. Earnings are $510. What were the sales, in dollars?

Adult only — reveal keys

1. \(350 + 0.08 \times 1250 = 350 + 100 = 450\).

2. \(350 + 0.08S = 510\), so \(0.08S = 160\), \(S = 2000\).

Catch: treating 8% as ×8, or taking 8% of the $350 instead of sales.
